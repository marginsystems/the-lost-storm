# Adding content

The game's content is data in `data/`. This page is the manual for it: what each file holds and how to add a skill, a monster, an item, a zone or a character. [README.md](../README.md) has the overview and [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) the rules the code keeps.

Content is data. None of these need a change to the game's logic; the one code edit is registering a new zone file in the content loader, described below:

- **A skill**: add an entry to `data/skills.json`. `delivery` picks how it hits: `arc` (melee sweep), `nova` (ring around the user) or `projectile`. `windup`, `recovery`, `cooldown` and `manaCost` set its timing and cost; `knockback`, `stagger`, `shake`, `hitStop` and `vfx` set how it feels; `hitStop` is how long the struck body holds its pose, kept between 40 and 80 ms and longer on a crit or a kill; the world never stops. `animation` names an action clip on the rig of whoever uses it. An optional `combo` (`window`, `steps`) makes the skill a chain: used again within `window` seconds of the last swing ending, it plays the next step instead, and each step has its own `animation`, `windup`, `recovery`, `stagger`, `shake` and `hitStop` and scales the skill's damage, area and knockback (`damageScale`, `areaScale`, `knockbackScale`). The steps scale the skill as the tree has changed it and keep its id, so passives, cooldown and sounds apply to the whole chain. Put it on the bar in `data/player.json` (`skillBar`, with key bindings) or give it to a monster.
- **A monster**: add an entry to `data/monsters.json` with its `rig`, stats, `behaviour` (`chaser` or `ranged`), `skill` and `loot`, then add it to a pack in a zone file. Its optional `poise` sets how it takes hits: `knockback` and `stagger` are the shares of a hit's knockback and stagger it takes, and `resolve` is how many of its windups in a row a hero may interrupt before its next one holds, so a hero cannot keep a heavy monster from ever swinging. A monster skill's optional `commit` (`at`, `breakStagger`) makes its windup committed once `at` of it has passed, or from its start once the monster's `resolve` is spent: then only a hit whose `stagger` is at least `breakStagger` interrupts it. Lighter hits still deal their damage. A swing that lands starts the count again.
- **Pack ranks**: `data/pack-ranks.json` lists the ranks a pack can roll when it spawns in a zone with `"ranks": true`: plain, magic and rare. Each has a name-plate `color`, an `aura` the members are tinted toward, a `chance` by monster level (`base`, `perLevel`, `max`), and multipliers for its `members` (`life`, `damage`, `xp`, `itemChance`, `gold`, `mods`). A rank with a `leader` gives one member of the pack, the one with the most life, its own multipliers, a name drawn from `names`, `mods` modifiers from the `pool`, and `items` guaranteed drops no lower than the `floor` rarity; the followers share the first of the leader's modifiers. Bosses, packs that already carry modifiers and the opening fight never roll a rank. A zone pack may name its rank with `"rank": "magic"`; it then spawns at that rank instead of rolling one. The rank check refuses a named rank that is unknown, that sits in a zone without ranks, or that sits on a pack with a boss, an already ranked monster or a modded member. A modifier in `data/monster-mods.json` scales life, damage, speed, size, xp and loot, and can also set `staggerScale` (the share of stagger it takes) and `regen` (the share of its life it heals each second).
- **The levelling pace**: `xp` in `data/player.json`. Level `L` needs `round(base × growth^(L−1))` experience, up to `maxLevel`. A kill gives the monster's `xp` scaled by its level (`xpPerLevel` in `data/world.json`; above the level named by `xpBeyond` there, the scale is the straight line's value at `from` times `lift`, growing by `growth` every level after it, so the time a level takes past the old cap follows the pace of the levels before it), and the Hall's monsters follow the hero's level up to the zone's `levelUpTo`. `gap` sets how a kill's experience depends on the monster's own level against the hero's: in full within `band` levels either way, plus one for every `widenEvery` hero levels; past that, `excess` levels beyond the band give a share of `1 / (1 + (excess / soft)^power)`, never below `floor`. With `above` off, monsters above the hero are never cut. A reduced gain is named by the experience bar, and the minimap names a wild zone with its level. A death costs at most a tenth of the current level and never a level. `npm run simulate -- farm` measures minutes and kills per level, and `node tools/level-pace-cli.ts` farms zones scaled to each level from 20 to 48 and reports minutes and kills per level by band, with and without `xpBeyond`.
- **An item**: add a base, an affix or a rarity to `data/items.json`. Its `slot` is one of `weapon`, `head`, `chest`, `hands`, `legs`, `feet` and `charm`. A weapon base's `visual` names the rig attachment to show in the hero's hand; an armour base's `wear` lists the wearables it puts on the body.
- **Clothing or armour**: add an entry to `data/wearables.json` (see below) and name it in an item base's `wear`, or in the `wear` list of the player or a monster to dress them with nothing equipped.
- **A skill-tree node**: add an entry to `data/skill-tree.json` with its grid position, what it `requires`, and its `statMods` and `skillMods`.
- **A sound**: `data/audio.json` maps game events to sound ids: by skill id (`skillStart`, `skillImpact`, `skillHit`, `projectileSpawn`, `projectileExpire`), by monster id or `player` (`hit`, `critHit`, `death`, `aggro`), by rarity or `gold` (`drop`, `pickup`), `notice`, `world` (`hero_step`, `level_up`, `surge`, `monster_spawn` and others), `ui` actions, and `ambience` per zone id. Each entry can set `volume`, `maxVoices` (copies of that sound at once) and `cooldown`. The sound files themselves are not in this repository (see [Sound](../README.md#sound) in the README).
- **A character to talk to**: add an entry to `data/npcs.json` (`name`, `title`, its `rig`, `tint`, `color` and held `gear`, and its `talks`), put its lines in `data/lines.json` as `{ id, speaker, text }`, and place it in a zone's `npcs` list. `talks` is an ordered list of conversations; the one that plays is the first that is repeatable or has not been heard yet, so list `once` conversations first and end with a repeatable one. A line id is stable: it is also the name of that line's recording, so add a new id rather than renaming one.
- **A boss**: give a monster a `boss` block: a `title`, `phases` (each `{ below, skills, barks }`, where `below` is the life fraction at which the phase begins, the first being 1, and `skills` is a weighted list), `poise` (`knockback`: the fraction of knockback it takes; a boss is never staggered or interrupted), a guaranteed `drop` (`items`, `rarity`, `goldMin`, `goldMax`) and `barks` (`aggro`, `death`): line ids from `data/lines.json` whose `speaker` is the monster's id. Boss skills are ordinary skills; give them a `windup` long enough to walk out of, because every monster attack draws its area on the ground while it winds up. Place the boss in a zone pack, and give the pack a `when` condition (for example its quest objective being `current`) so it is not handed out again once beaten.
- **A quest**: add an entry to `data/quests.json`: a `name`, the quests it `requires`, and its `objectives` in order. An objective has an `id`, the `text` the tracker shows, and a `type`: `talk` (finish a conversation with `npc`), `reach` (stand in a circle in a `zone`) or `defeat` (kill a `monster` of that kind). Anything can depend on progress through one small condition, `{ "type": "quest", "quest", "is": locked | active | done }` or `{ "type": "objective", "quest", "objective", "is": pending | current | done }`: a conversation's `when`, an NPC placement's `when`, a pack's `when`, a prop's `when`, a zone voice's `when`, and an exit's `requires` (with a `lockedHint` shown when the exit is refused). Add `"not": true` to a condition to turn it round, for something that is there until an objective is done. Give every `talk` objective a `once` conversation whose `when` is that objective being `current`. Any objective can carry `when` (conditions that must hold before it can be met), `waiting` (the line the tracker shows while `when` does not hold) and `sets` (story flags set when it is met). Leave `text` empty to let the tracker name the step from the data: `Go to` the next zone, then `Talk to` the person or `Defeat` the monster by name. An objective of type `either` holds two or more `paths`, each an ordinary objective with its own `when` and `sets`; the first path met completes it, so one step of a quest can be done in more than one way, and the flags a path sets record which. A flag condition is `{ "type": "flag", "flag", "is" }`; `is` may be left out to mean the flag is set at all.
- **A waypoint**: give a zone a `waypoint` (`x`, `z`, `radius`, or `null` for none) and a place on its act's map (below). Standing on a waypoint unlocks it; the map (M) then teleports between unlocked waypoints.
- **An act and its map**: `data/world.json` lists `acts`. Each act has a `title`, a `name` and an `atlas`: the sheet's `width` and `height`, a `places` entry per zone (`zone`, `x`, `y`), a `roads` entry per pair of zones joined by an exit (`from`, `to` and the `via` points the road bends through), the terrain `features` the sheet is drawn from (`coast` is a closed land outline; `ridge` and `river` are lines; `forest` and `marsh` are closed regions), region `labels` and the `compass` position. Every zone belongs to exactly one act, and the map shown is the act of the zone the hero stands in. An optional `end` (`when`, `line`) ends the act: the moment its conditions first hold, an end-of-act card shows the act's title and the one plain `line`. An optional top-level `retired` (`zones`, `to`, `flags`) lists zone ids that no longer exist: a save standing in one of them loads in the zone `to`, at its start, with `flags` added and the character otherwise untouched. A zone is drawn as a town, a wild zone, a boss lair or a void from its `kind` and its packs, so a new act with a different map is data only. Every place is named and every road is drawn from the first open: a place the hero has not reached is named in dim ink, and a road is lit only once the hero has visited both its ends.
- **A zone**: add `data/zones/<id>.json`, then import it in `sim/content/content.ts` and add it to the `zones` list of `RAW_CONTENT` there (a test fails when a zone file is not loaded). A zone holds the tile map (4-unit cells), wall and floor models, prop placements, the player start, the monster packs and their `level`, its `kind` (`town` is safe and has no packs; `wild` has monsters), optional `surge` rules for endless surges, `checkpoints`, its `ambience` (fog, sky and key light) and its `exits`. An exit names the zone and the exit on the other side (`to`, `toExit`) and the `arrival` point where a hero coming through it stands; the two exits must name each other. `data/world.json` names the start zone and how monsters scale with level. A zone can also change with the story:
  - A prop with a `when` is drawn only while its condition holds, and its light eases in and out. It is never part of the walkable map, so it must not be a prop that blocks.
  - A pack with a `when` appears when its condition starts to hold, also while the hero is already in the zone, once per visit.
  - An exit with `"hiddenWhenLocked": true` has no marker and no label in the zone until its `requires` holds. The world map still draws its road, dimmed, so the act's whole route reads from the first open.
  - `voices` are spoken lines tied to a place: `{ id, x, z, radius, lines, when }`. The first time the hero steps into the circle the lines play as subtitles without stopping the game, once per save. A voice id must not be the id of a conversation.
  - `walls` may be `null`: the floor then ends without wall models or corner pillars, and its edge fades out over the `outside` surface. The walkable map still ends where the floor tiles end.
  - A surface may set `pace` (default 1): the share of normal speed for anyone walking or rolling over it, so `0.5` is wading. Paths prefer faster ground when the way round is cheaper.
  - `drifts` are rectangles of floor cells that come and go: `{ id, column, row, columns, rows, period, open, offset, fade }`. Each is walkable for the `open` share of every `period` seconds (shifted by `offset`), fades in over `fade` seconds after it settles and fades out over the last `fade` seconds before it goes. Anyone standing on it when it goes is set down on the nearest firm ground. A drift must not cover the start, an exit, an arrival point, a checkpoint or the waypoint.
  - `triggers` are places that set story flags: `{ id, x, z, radius, when, sets }`. The hero stepping into one while its `when` holds sets its flags, which is how a scene, a glitch or a branch is hung on a spot in the data. Props, NPCs, packs and exits with a `when` on those flags then come and go.
  - `moments` is a moment-strike field, or absent for none: `{ combat, areas, clearance, interval, start, max, table }`. Lightning lands somewhere inside one of the `areas` (`{ x, z, radius }` circles in zone metres, larger ones chosen more often) every `interval.min` to `interval.max` seconds while fewer than `max` bubbles are live; `start` bubbles are already there when the hero arrives. A strike keeps `clearance.hero` metres between its bubble's edge and the hero, `clearance.exits` from every exit and `clearance.between` from other bubbles. `table` is a weighted list `{ moment, weight }` of ids from `data/moments.json`. With `"combat": false` (the black shore) a bubble plays when walked into and fades, nothing turns and nothing drops.
  - A moment in `data/moments.json` has a `name`, a colour `tint`, `bright` for the rare richer ones, a bubble `radius`, a dormant `lifetime`, how long it stays `awake`, its set-piece `props` (`{ prop, x, z, rotationDegrees }` from the bubble centre; drawn only, never blocking) and its `echoes` (`{ monster, x, z, facingDegrees, pose: { clip, at }, turns }`: an archetype, where it stands, the clip and the point in it where it is frozen, and whether it may turn). An echo may add a `look` (`{ rig, scale, gear, wear }`) to be drawn, and to fight, with another body and clothes than its archetype, such as plain townsfolk or a child at `scale` 0.6; an echo that may turn needs its archetype's fighting clip on that rig. On waking each echo with `turns` turns hostile with the moment's `turnChance`; turned echoes fight with their archetype's behaviour as members of the `faction` named at the top of the file, and drop nothing themselves. When every turned echo is dead the `reward` (`goldMin`, `goldMax`, up to `items` items at `itemChance` each, an optional `rarity`, all scaled by zone level) drops at the bubble centre; if the `awake` time runs out first they dissolve and nothing drops. A strike's ground mark lasts `telegraph` seconds and a finished bubble dissolves over `fade` seconds; it never hurts the hero.
  - Keep packs away from the start, arrival points, checkpoints and the waypoint: a test fails when a pack notices the hero standing on one of them.

If something cannot be expressed in the data, extend the schema in `sim/content/` in a general way first. See [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## Characters and gear

Humans stand on the Quaternius universal skeleton (`pelvis`, `spine_01`, `hand_r`, `index_01_r` and so on). A body is `public/models/characters/<frame>.glb`, a head with eyes and brows, and the rig names that frame in `frame`. Any body with the same joint names plays the clips in `public/models/animations/universal/`, because `src/render/clip-fit.ts` fits each clip to the body's rest pose when it loads. The skeleton monsters keep the older Universal Animation Library rig (`DEF-*` joints) and the clips in `public/models/animations/clips/`.

A character is a rig entry in `data/rigs.json`. The loader (`src/render/character-loader.ts`) needs nothing else.

```json
{
  "id": "skeleton_hero",
  "url": "models/characters/skeleton.glb",
  "clipFolder": "models/animations/clips",
  "scale": 1.2,
  "yawOffsetDegrees": 0,
  "height": 1.85,
  "rootMotionBone": null,
  "gaits": [
    { "clip": "Walk_Loop", "groundSpeed": 1.14 },
    { "clip": "Jog_Fwd_Loop", "groundSpeed": 7.08 }
  ],
  "clips": {
    "idle": "Sword_Idle",
    "run": "Jog_Fwd_Loop",
    "hit": ["Hit_Chest", "Hit_Head"],
    "death": ["Death01", "Death_Knockback", "Death_Collapse"],
    "actions": {
      "attack": { "clip": "Sword_Combo_A", "impactFraction": 0.18 },
      "attack_b": { "clip": "Sword_Combo_B", "impactFraction": 0.17 },
      "slam": { "clip": "Sword_Slam", "impactFraction": 0.245 },
      "cast": { "clip": "Spell_Simple_Shoot", "impactFraction": 0.35 }
    }
  },
  "sockets": {
    "hand_r": { "bone": "DEF-hand.R", "position": [-0.03, 0.085, 0], "rotationDegrees": [90, 90, 0], "scale": 1 }
  },
  "hiddenNodes": [],
  "bodyParts": {
    "feet": ["DEF-foot.L", "DEF-toe.L", "DEF-foot.R", "DEF-toe.R"]
  }
}
```

- `url` is the body. `clipFolder` is the folder that holds one GLB per clip, named after the clip, so one set of clip files serves every body on the same rig and only the clips a rig names are loaded.
- `clips` names the clips in that folder. `idle` and `run` are one clip each. `hit` and `death` are lists, and one is picked at random each time, so a pack does not fall the same way. `actions` is the set of attack clips this rig can play, under names of your choosing: a skill's `animation` (and each combo step's) is one of those names, and the content check reports a skill given to a character whose rig lacks it. `impactFraction` is the point in the clip where the blow lands; the game speeds the clip up or down so that point meets the skill's `windup`, then plays the rest over the `recovery`. If a clip name is wrong, the error lists the clips the files do contain.
- `gaits` lists the walking and running clips from slowest to fastest, each with `groundSpeed`, the speed in metres per second the planted foot travels at normal playback on a body of this rig's `scale`. The renderer plays the gait nearest a character's actual speed, at the rate that makes the foot cover exactly the ground the body covers, and plays `idle` whenever the body is not moving. Measure a new clip's ground speed from its stance foot rather than guessing it.
- Every way a character moves maps to a clip: walking plays a gait, a dash or lunge plays its action, a knockback plays a `hit` clip with a short hop and lean back for as long as the push lasts. Anyone in the middle of an attack keeps their feet.
- `rootMotionBone` pins that bone's horizontal movement so a clip with root motion plays in place.
- `sockets` are named mounting points: a bone plus an offset, rotation and scale.
- `hiddenNodes` are nodes in the body file that are never shown.
- `bodyParts` names groups of joints. The body's triangles skinned mostly to a group are left undrawn while a wearable that `hides` that part is worn.
- `frame` names the body's frame (`man` or `woman`), which picks the clothes made for it.

Held gear is an entry in `data/attachments.json`: an id, a model file, the socket it mounts on, and `surfaces`, which dresses each of the model's named materials (`steel`, `leather`, `wood`) in a surface from `data/surfaces.json` the way a prop is dressed. An item base's `visual`, the player's `gear` and a monster's `gear` name attachment ids, so a new weapon model is one GLB and one line of data. Weapon models are authored with the grip at the origin and the blade along +Y. `tint` on the player and on each monster recolours the body, so one body file makes several characters. `color` tints that character's effects and its body's emissive map (glowing eyes, runes) when the body has one; a body without an emissive map does not glow.

Clothing and armour are wearables: modular skinned parts in `public/models/wear/<frame>/`, bound to the wearer's own skeleton by joint name, so a part fits every body of its frame and bends with every clip. An entry in `data/wearables.json` has:

```json
{ "id": "dirty_shirt", "model": "models/wear/{frame}/peasant.glb", "pieces": ["Peasant_Body", "Peasant_Arms"], "region": "chest", "tint": "#9c927c", "hides": [] }
```

- `model` is the outfit file, with `{frame}` replaced by the wearer's frame, and `pieces` names the parts of it this wearable shows.
- `region` is the part of the body it covers: `head`, `shoulders`, `chest`, `arms`, `hands`, `legs` or `feet`. A character shows one piece per region. Its own clothes (`wear` on the player or a monster) come first, and each equipped item's pieces replace whatever covers the same region (`sim/wear.ts`).
- `tint` multiplies the piece's colour, so one model gives a clean shirt, a dirty one and a monster's rags.
- `hides` lists the wearer's `bodyParts` that are not drawn under it, so a body does not poke through a piece that hugs it.

New bodies and outfits are assembled from the Quaternius packs by `tools/assemble-body.ts` from `tools/universal-bodies.json`. [ASSETS.md](../ASSETS.md) has the commands.

A held weapon sits in its socket, and the fingers close on it only through the authored clips.

An armour base also names its `material`: `cloth`, `leather`, `mail` or `plate`. Within a slot a heavier material gives more armour, and turns up later and less often. No item base has a negative line: an item only ever adds. `sim/items.test.ts` holds the data to both.

To use a different rig, add its rig entry with its own bone names in `sockets`, and point `data/player.json` (`rig`) or a monster at it. Mixamo characters work this way (export FBX, convert to GLB, set `scale` to 0.01 and `rootMotionBone` to `mixamorigHips`), but Mixamo files may not be redistributed, so they go in `public/models/local/`, which is git-ignored, and never in this repository.
