# Lost Storm RL

Trains our own model to play The Lost Storm through the headless simulation. The game stays in TypeScript (`sim/`); this folder is the Python trainer (PyTorch) that drives it.

## How it fits together

```
Python (rl/)                         Node (tools/rl/serve.ts, one process per worker)
  PPO / imitation  <-- binary frames over stdin/stdout -->  pool of arenas (sim/rl/pool.ts)
  Policy network                                             observe -> numbers (sim/rl/observe.ts)
                                                             action  -> commands (sim/rl/act.ts)
                                                             stepWorld, reward (sim/rl/reward.ts)
```

- **Arena** (`sim/rl/arena.ts`): the Hall of Time with its own monsters cleared, a bench hero with a random level (1-12, or the range `ppo --levels` sets), weapon and skill bar (or a fixed build, see Builds), and five waves of random packs. Every episode jitters skill damage, cooldowns, mana costs and monster life, damage and speed by up to 20%, so the model learns to read numbers instead of memorising them.
- **Decisions**: the model acts every 4 sim ticks (7.5 times a second). Each action is four choices: move (stay or one of 8 directions), skill (none or one of 6 slots), target (one of the 8 nearest foes), use (none, one of 2 flasks, or pick up the nearest thing on the ground the pickup rule wants and the bag can hold).
- **Pickup rule**: each arena rolls a lowest rarity worth taking (from "take everything" up to "take nothing but gold"). The hero sees the rule and its free bag space, and every loot token carries its rarity, its gold amount, whether the rule wants it and whether it fits. After each wave a few items of random rarity fall near the hero, so the model learns to take what the rule asks for and walk past the rest. A player's own loot filter will feed the same input.
- **Statuses and foes** (`sim/rl/status.ts`): the hero reads its own burn still to come, brand, slow and weakening, and how many foes are coming for it. Each foe token adds the same statuses, whether it is coming for the hero (not held on another foe), its root, and what makes it dangerous by numbers: pack leader, how many mods, a burst on death, summons, regeneration, a guard, and its recovery window and strain after heavy hits.
- **Reward**: damage dealt as a share of the foe's life, +0.5 a kill, +2 a wave, +3 for clearing all waves, +0.1 for picking up gold with no foe within 6 m, and +0.1 x (1 + its rarity share) for an item the rule wants; damage taken as a share of the hero's life x2, -3 for dying, and a small cost per decision. After the last wave the arena stays open up to 5 s, or until nothing the rule wants is left, so there is time to loot.
- **Dodging**: the hero reads, for standing still and each of the 8 move directions, whether a blow winding up, a delayed strike, a hazard or burning ground will catch it there (`sim/rl/danger.ts`, the same check the scripted bot dodges by). It also reads its own last two decisions (each head as a one-hot), the damage it took and dealt in the last second, how long since it was last hit and whether that hit came from something out of view, and up to four foes it saw in the last 5 s that dropped out of its view, at their last seen spot (`sim/rl/recall.ts`). Entity slots are sorted by distance, so raw past snapshots would not line up; these features are worked out in the sim instead.

## The contract, and why game updates do not throw training away

`sim/rl/contract.ts` is the versioned contract between the game and the model: every input the model sees and every action it can take. Its fingerprint is pinned in `sim/rl/contract.test.ts`.

- Skills, monsters, projectiles and areas are described by their numbers (reach, area, damage, cooldown, delivery type, behaviour), never by their id. A buff or a nerf changes the numbers the model already reads. A new skill looks like a skill with numbers it has not tried yet. Keep training from the last checkpoint.
- The model reads the hero, each skill slot and each nearby thing as separate tokens through a small transformer. It picks a skill by scoring the slot's own token and a target by scoring the foe's token, so nothing is tied to a slot position or a monster name.
- The situation (`arena`, `duel`, `quest`, `roam`) and an objective direction are inputs from the start, so one model can learn every mode. `rival` (another player), `ally` (a hero on its own side) and `objective` are already entity kinds, which is why roams and escorts needed no new input.
- Each block has spare columns. A new feature can take a spare column without changing the shape.
- Changing the layout (a new delivery type, more slots) changes the fingerprint and fails the test. Raise `RL.version`, update the pinned fingerprint, and widen saved policies so their weights carry over.

## Train

```bash
uv run python -m lost_storm_rl.train imitate --run arena-v1
uv run python -m lost_storm_rl.train ppo --run arena-v1 --start runs/arena-v1/imitate.pt --updates 300
uv run python -m lost_storm_rl.train eval --run arena-v1 --policy runs/arena-v1/latest.pt --episodes 32
```

- `imitate` records the scripted bot (`sim/bot.ts`) playing arenas and trains the policy to copy it, so PPO starts from something that already fights.
- `ppo` improves it by playing. Each update prints the win rate, deaths, mean return and waves cleared, and appends a row to `runs/<run>/log.csv`. Checkpoints go to `runs/<run>/policy-uNNNNN.pt` and `runs/<run>/latest.pt`.
- `ppo` keeps learning from the scripted bot while it plays. Every `--bot-every` updates (20) the workers play `--bot-episodes` arenas (16) with the bot and keep the last `--bot-keep` runs (4). Each minibatch adds the loss of copying the bot's choices there, weighted `--bot-coef` (0.5) and fading over `--bot-decay` updates (3000) to `--bot-floor` (0.05). PPO alone drifted below its teacher: on the same 64 arenas the bot won 91% and the model 45%. The anchor keeps it near the bot while PPO improves on it.
- `--curriculum` (0.5) draws each arena's hero level by how often the model still loses there: that share of the draw follows each level's running loss rate, and the rest is even across the arena levels (1-12, or `--levels`). The running win rate from level 9 up (or at the top level of a range that ends below 9) is in `log.csv` as `win_high`, and `bot_loss` and `bot_weight` show the anchor. `--curriculum 0` and `--bot-coef 0` turn them off.
- A flask cannot be chosen when drinking it would be refused (a full pool, no charges, cooling down), so the model no longer spends choices on it.
- `ppo --levels 1-20` trains over a wider range of hero levels than the default 1-12, up to the top of the experience curve (30). Duels without builds draw their level from the same range, and the curriculum, the bot anchor and the best check follow it. `eval --levels` plays its arenas over a range the same way. The result groups episodes in bands of four levels (`levels 1-4`, `levels 5-8`, ...) up to the highest level played, and the best check reports the top band.
- What the model sees of the hero's armour is the share of a hit it takes away (`armourReduction` with the cap in `data/player.json`), so it means the same at every level and never reaches the observation's clip.
- `ppo --duels N` gives N duels to each worker. Each duel takes two of that worker's `--envs`, one seat per hero, and the same policy plays both sides (self-play). The two heroes get random builds at the same level, start apart in an empty arena, and are in rival parties. The duel ends when one falls, which earns the winner `REWARD.win`, or when `DUEL.timeLimit` runs out. Win rate, deaths, return and waves count arena episodes only. Duels are logged on their own, per seat, so one finished duel counts twice: how many seats finished, the share decided before time ran out, and their mean return (`duel_episodes`, `duel_decided`, `duel_return` in `log.csv`). A run whose `log.csv` has an older header moves it aside as `log-N.csv` and starts a new one.
- `ppo --corner-duels S` starts that share of the duels (0 to 1, by the duel's seed) as a corner drill (`sim/rl/corner.ts`). The rival seat stands in a corner of the arena (a spot walled in on at least 9 of the 16 ray directions within 3 m, found anywhere on the floor, so corners face every way), and the first seat stands 4.5 to 7 m out in the open, turned up to 0.6 rad off the corner's open direction, with Storm Bolt in slot 1. The first seat is not the model's to steer: its mask leaves one choice in each head, Storm Bolt at the cornered hero whenever it can be cast and nothing otherwise, so its samples carry no policy gradient. Its life and mana are kept full, so it cannot be worn down from the corner. The cornered hero wins the drill (`REWARD.win`) by getting 4 m clear of its corner while no farther from the caster than it started, so it learns to come out to fight rather than slip away along the wall. Getting more than 3 m farther from the caster than it started loses the drill as a death (`REWARD.death`), so running out of Storm Bolt's range is never cheaper than coming out; still there after 15 s, both seats pay the stalemate. Cornered seats are logged on their own: how many finished, the share that got clear and the share that ran out of time (`corner_episodes`, `corner_won`, `corner_timeout`). A replay keeps the corner, so it plays the same drill again.
- `ppo --roams N --escorts N` gives each worker N roams (one env each) and N escorts (two envs each). A roam (`sim/rl/roam.ts`) puts the hero in a real zone with that zone's own packs, set to the hero's level, a walk of 20 to 40 m away from a goal with a pack on the way. The hero reads the `roam` situation and an objective: the next corner of the game's own path to the goal, so it steers by a point it can walk straight at. It is paid as in an arena for its own blows, kills and hits taken, 0.05 a metre of path covered, and `REWARD.win` for arriving; the roam ends when it arrives, falls, or runs out of time (40 s plus 2 s a metre). Only the monsters within 26 m of the route are kept, which keeps a step as cheap as an arena's. An escort is a roam with a second hero in the lead's party, played by the same policy. The partner's objective is the lead. It is paid for its own blows, a little for every decision within 8 m of the lead and charged beyond 16 m, charged half of what the lead is hit for, and paid 1.5 when the lead arrives with it near. Either hero falling ends the roam for both, and the one left standing is charged half a death. Roams and escorts are logged on their own, per seat: how many seats finished, the share of leads that arrived, the share of partners that arrived with them, and their mean return (`roam_episodes`, `roam_won`, `partner_won`, `roam_return`). The envs come after the arenas and duels, so a run without them is laid out as before. Levels, `--build` and `--tree-builds` apply as in an arena (the partner takes an example build in turn, or its own tree build). The bot anchor, the curriculum and the best check still read arenas only, and a roam keeps no replay yet. The first roam in each zone builds that zone's walk grid, a few seconds a zone for each worker.
- `eval --roams` and `eval --escorts` play `--episodes` of them with a checkpoint and print how the leads and the partners ended.
- `ppo --gathers N` gives each worker N gathers (one env each, after the escorts). A gather (`sim/rl/gather.ts`) teaches walking among people: the Hideout half the time, otherwise another open zone (no duel, no void), with every monster taken out. The hero walks at a walking pace drawn from 1.3 to 2.2 m/s (his `move_speed`), and 1 to 8 allied heroes stand about him (`sim/rl/bystanders.ts`): alone, in small talking groups facing their middle, wandering between stops, or crossing his way at about the time he gets there. The goal is one of three: walk to a spot and stop there; meet a person (the objective is that bystander, and he must stop 1.3 to 2.2 m from them, still and facing them); or stand where he is and face a point (`progress` is 1, the objective is the point, or none at all, which means stay put). It reads the `roam` situation, so the contract does not move, and only moves can be chosen: skills and flasks are masked. It is paid 0.1 a metre toward the goal and for turning toward the person or point once there, and `REWARD.win` for holding the goal for two decisions (twelve for standing with no point). It is charged for coming within 1 m (body edge to edge) of a bystander, more the closer, and 0.3 m of the person it meets; more again for touching; 0.3 for walking between two people within 3.5 m who face each other; and 0.02 each time it turns back the other way within two decisions. Gathers are logged on their own: how many finished, the share won and by goal, the share with any contact, and the mean personal-space intrusions, crossings and jitters (`gather_episodes`, `gather_won`, `gather_contact`, `gather_intrusions`, `gather_crossings`, `gather_jitters`, `gather_return`, `spot_won`, `meet_won`, `face_won`). `eval --gathers` plays `--episodes` of them and prints the same.
- The Wayfarer's legs are `sim/rl/resident-pilot.ts`: a server keeps one `createResidentPilot` per resident, tells it where the players are (`see`), what it wants (`want`: go to a spot, meet a player, or stand and face a point) and steps it about 30 times a second (`step`). It keeps its own world of the zone with the players as allied bodies, builds the gather observation, asks the model every 4 ticks with only moves open, and returns the pose. With no model it follows the path itself. `residentPilotFrom` reads an exported file's bytes; export a gather run with `export --policy runs/gather-v1/latest.pt --out ../public/models/rl/resident-pilot.bin`.
- `eval` plays greedy episodes with a checkpoint and keeps every one as a replay, then lets the bot play the same arenas and prints both side by side (`against_bot`: won overall and by level band, and how many the bot won that the model lost). `--sample` plays as training does, with sampled choices. `eval --duels` plays `--episodes` duels instead, the checkpoint on both sides, and prints how each seat ended and the share decided before time ran out.
- `match --first a.pt --second b.pt` puts two different checkpoints in a duel. Each of `--duels` arenas (128) is played twice with the seats swapped, so neither model gains from a side, and it prints how many each won, how many timed out, and the split by seat. Its seeds start at 3002000000 (held out, so neither model trained on them), and its replays go to `runs/<run>/match/`.
- `match --first a.pt --first-build a.json --second b.pt --second-build b.json` gives each checkpoint its own build (see Builds). Each model keeps its build when the seats swap, and the result names `first_build`, `second_build` and the `level` they fought at (20 unless `--level` says otherwise). Leave the build flags out and both seats get random bench heroes, as before.
- Every `--best-every` updates (200; 0 turns it off) training plays `--best-episodes` greedy arenas (64) against the bot the same way and saves the model as `best.pt` when it wins a larger share of them than the model already in `best.pt`, whose score is stored inside it, so a rerun into the same folder has to beat it too. `best.pt` is replaced in one step, and each check is then logged as a row in `runs/<run>/best.csv`. Checkpoints swing by twenty points or more between updates, so `best.pt`, not `latest.pt`, is the model to show.
- Training gives each new arena the next seed from `--seed`, so a long run plays every low seed. The game reads a seed as 32 bits, so a seed of 2^32 or more builds the same arena as a low one. Seeds from 3000000000 up are held out: `ppo` refuses a `--seed` and `--updates` whose arenas, or the bot's, would reach them, the best check picks from 3001000000 (`--best-seed`), and a model is reported with `eval --seed 3000000000`, a range the pick never saw.
- Replays are JSON files in `runs/<run>/replays/` and `runs/<run>/eval/`: the arena seed plus the action list, or for a duel the duel seed plus each seat's action list. The sim is deterministic, so a replay plays the same fight again (`replayArena` in `sim/rl/replay.ts`, `replayDuel` in `sim/rl/duel-replay.ts`). Training keeps a duel on the `--keep-every` schedule.
- Watch a replay in the real game: run `npm run dev` and open `http://localhost:5193/play/?replay=/rl/runs/<run>/eval/<file>.json`. Add `&speed=2` (0.25 to 8) to play it faster. The watch mode exists only in the dev server; it saves nothing and takes no input, and a caption in the top-left corner names the replay, the wave and the outcome. A duel replay draws both heroes and frames them both: the camera aims between them and pulls back as they spread, never nearer than the play view. The play HUD gives way to two life bars named for the models (a `match-first<n>` label says which seat the first model sits in), a tag over each hero, the time left, and at the end who won.

## Builds

A build is a named hero at the fixed duel level, so a duel is a contest of build and brain. It is a JSON file. This small one is legal as it stands; the files in `rl/builds/` spend every point:

```json
{ "name": "Spin", "weapon": "warden_broadsword", "skills": ["thunderhead"], "route": ["blade_thunderhead", "heavy_hands", "blade_1"], "hero": "woman" }
```

- `name` is what the result prints. `hero` is optional (one of the heroes in `data/player.json`); it changes only the look.
- `weapon` is one of the standard weapons in `data/duel-kit.json`: one plain base for each weapon group (blade, heavy, crossbow, staff), chosen for a similar plain hit rate. Every hero also wears the same plain armour set listed there. All gear is common, has no affixes and is at the hero's item level.
- `route` is the skill-tree places in the order they are taken. Every hero is level 20 (`BUILD.level` in `sim/rl/build.ts`) and has 38 points, so the route takes at most 38 places. `--level N` on `check`, `ppo`, `eval` and `match` checks and plays builds at another level, with `2 × (N - 1)` points; it needs a build (bench heroes take `--levels`). In a build run `win_high` is the win rate at the build's level. The first must be a starting place and each next one must join the places already taken, within the specialty limit, the same rules as allocating in the game.
- `skills` are up to four skills for slots 1-4. The basic attack is always slot 0 and the roll slot 5. Each skill must be in the skill book or unlocked by a place on the route.
- Nothing is random once a build is given: the same build always gives the same hero.
- `train check --build x.json` says whether a build is legal and, if not, everything that is wrong with it. `ppo`, `eval` and `match` refuse an illegal build the same way before they start.
- `ppo --build x.json` plays that build in every arena (at the duel level, so `--curriculum` does not move it) and in the first seat of every duel. The bot anchor and the best check play the same build. The other duel seat takes the example builds in `rl/builds/` in turn (any that is no longer legal is skipped; with none left it is a random bench hero).
- `eval --build x.json` plays its arenas with the build; `eval --duels --build x.json` puts it in the first seat against the example builds in turn, and `first_seat_outcomes` shows how it did.
- Replays keep the builds, so a replay plays the same heroes again.
- `rl/builds/` holds five example builds, derived from the routes in `tools/simulate-policies.json`: Maelstrom and Duelist (blade), Bulwark (heavy), Windrunner (crossbow) and Caster (staff). `sim/rl/build.examples.test.ts` checks that each one is legal.

## Training on tree builds

The model is a pilot: it should fight well with any build, and a separate search picks the tree for it.

- `ppo --tree-builds --levels 1-30 --level-lean 1` gives every arena and both seats of every duel a random legal skill-tree build at its level (`sim/rl/tree-build.ts`). The weapon is one of the standard weapons in `data/duel-kit.json`. The route grows one place at a time by the same rules as allocating in the game, from a starting place, through places that join the ones taken, within the specialty limit, and spends all `2 × (level - 1)` points. Places tied to another weapon group than the one held are drawn less often (`TREE_BUILD.offWeapon`), places that unlock a skill more often (`TREE_BUILD.unlocks`). The bar takes one to four skills from the skill book and the route's unlocks. Nothing in the code names a place or a skill, so a changed tree needs no code change.
- A tree build and its level follow from the arena's seed (and the level weights), so the same seed always gives the same hero and a replay keeps its build. The bot anchor and the best check play tree builds the same way, so the paired comparison with the bot is on the same heroes.
- `--level-lean` (0) weights the even share of the level draw by level to that power, so `--level-lean 1` plays level 30 thirty times as often as level 1 before the curriculum's own lean toward the levels the model loses. It works with `--curriculum 0` too.
- `eval --tree-builds --levels 1-30` measures a checkpoint on tree builds.
- What a tree changes reaches the model by numbers: the slot tokens already carry the resolved skill (damage, area, cooldown, range, cost, knockback, stagger, projectiles, chain jumps), and contract v7 adds the hero's `damage_percent`, `crit_multiplier`, `life_regen`, `mana_regen`, `storm_resist`, `frost_resist` and how many on-event `powers` it holds, and each slot's `life_on_hit`, `leech`, `pierce`, `echo` and `momentum`. Contract v8 adds the hero's `knock_left` (seconds it still slides from a shove) and `knock_grace` (seconds before another hero's shove can move it again), so a hero under Storm Bolt fire can time its way out.

## Build search

```bash
uv run python -m lost_storm_rl.train search --policy runs/trees-v1/best.pt --level 20 --budget 40
```

- `search` looks for the strongest build for a trained model at one level. It starts from the example builds in `rl/builds/` that are legal at that level and `--randoms` random tree builds (12), then repeatedly takes the `--keep` best so far (4) and changes each by one legal step: refund a place that can go and take another that joins, change a bar skill, or change the weapon (`mutateBuild` in `sim/rl/tree-build.ts`). The builds come from `tools/rl/build-tool.ts`, which runs the same generator and validator as training.
- Each candidate is scored by greedy play: `--duels` duels a seat (4) against each example build, both seats, plus `--arenas` arenas (16) with the build. Its score is the share of all of those it won. Every candidate plays the same seeds, from 2800000000 (`--seed`): training refuses seeds that reach them, and they stay below 3000000000, where the judge's seeds start.
- `--budget` (40) bounds the candidates scored, `--workers` (1, at most 2) scores that many at once. Every candidate is logged with its score and build to `runs/<run>/search-L<level>/search.csv`, and a rerun into the same folder resumes from that log without scoring a build twice.
- The best build is written to `runs/<run>/search-L<level>/best.json`, named with its score, ready for `check`, `ppo --build`, `eval --build` and `match`.
- When the tree changes, nothing here needs code: retrain from the latest checkpoint (widened first if the contract moved), then rerun the search.

Everything under `runs/` is ignored by git.

## Export a model to the game

The game runs a trained model itself, in the browser, from one small file.

```bash
uv run python -m lost_storm_rl.train export --policy runs/levels20-v2/best-v7.pt --out ../public/models/rl/duel-pilot.bin --fixture ../sim/rl/policy-net.fixture.json
```

- The file is the 4 bytes `LSPL`, a u32 header length, a JSON header, padding to an even offset, then every tensor as float16 in header order. The header names the contract (`version`, `fingerprint`, `layout`, `heads`), `width`, `layers`, `attentionHeads`, each tensor's `name` and `shape`, and the checkpoint's `update` and best-check score.
- The value head is left out (the game only acts), and float16 halves the rest, so a 2 MB checkpoint becomes about 1 MB.
- `export` refuses a checkpoint for another contract than the game's; widen it first.
- `--fixture` plays a few greedy duels on held-out seeds and writes their observations (non-zero numbers only), masks, the float16 model's logits and its greedy actions. The game's TypeScript forward pass is tested against it. The printed `same_greedy_action` is the share of those observations where the float16 model chooses exactly what the checkpoint chooses.
- The shipped file is our own work and has its row in `ASSETS.md`. Never commit the `.pt`.
